# Problem statement

## The user

A patient, often an older adult or the family member helping them, who has found a clinical study on ClinicalTrials.gov that might help them.

## The problem

Finding a listed study is not the hard part. Getting from "this looks relevant" to a real conversation with the study team is where most interested patients are lost.

**Most patient inquiries never turn into a referral.** In a 2024 pilot, 133 patients with cancer were referred to trial sites through a patient-facing trial finder (Ancora.ai, with Tufts CSDD; 17 countries, Sept 2021 – June 2024):

| Stage | Patients (of 133) | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Got any response using only the ClinicalTrials.gov contact details | 46 | 35% |
| Got any response after adding LinkedIn and other channels | 62 | 47% |
| Got actionable information | 53 | 40% |
| Got accurate information that let them proceed | 26 | 20% |
| Were successfully referred | 7 | 5% |

- About half of interested patients never received a reply.
- Nine replies said the trial was no longer recruiting, although it was listed as recruiting.
- Two of the seven successful referrals relied on contact details found outside ClinicalTrials.gov.

*Limits:* a small, single-platform pilot of cancer patients; the results may not generalize to other conditions.

**Listings are hard to understand.**
- Only 12% of US adults are proficient in health literacy.
- A review of ClinicalTrials.gov listings found the information "very difficult to digest", text-heavy and rarely in plain language.
- Key facts a patient cares about are buried in technical wording. For example, "2:1 randomization … sham control" means a 1-in-3 chance of receiving a placebo procedure.

**Older adults face extra barriers.**
- Trial teams in six large European trials (7,612 patients aged 65 and over) ranked impaired health and comorbidities as the top recruitment barrier and the top reason for dropping out.
- They also cited travel and visit burden, long study duration, and patient information that was too detailed or unsuitable for people with sensory or cognitive impairment.
- Many trials exclude older adults outright through upper age limits or exclusions such as blood thinners, prior surgery, or "poor general health". Patients often find out only after reading dense criteria or after a screening call.

## What Treatable does about it

- **Understand it:** plain-language facts about what participation involves (placebo or sham chance, visits, duration, procedures), drawn from the public record.
- **Check the fit:** eligibility criteria turned into yes / no / not sure questions. The patient's answers stay on their device.
- **Reach the team:** the right contact, a ready-to-send inquiry, questions for the uncertain criteria, flags such as a near-term completion date or inconsistent contact details, and a reminder to follow up when there is no reply.
